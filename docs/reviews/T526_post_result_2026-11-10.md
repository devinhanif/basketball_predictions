## 3. What the checks mean

### 3.1 The within-date label shuffle is not a valid null here (an invalid test is not a null)
Shuffling y within date gives a pts and reb "gain" larger than the real one (-0.0347 vs -0.0232). S - U is a shift-and-widen
contrast. Against labels drawn at random from the night's pool, the arm with the higher, wider grid wins whatever the row
identity. A null that cannot return 0 under H0 says nothing. The valid null for this design is the placebo sitter (1b): same
model, same kind of team-game, vacancy that is false. It reverses. Recommend: future scenario rules declare a placebo-sitter
null, not a label shuffle (draft BEST_PRACTICES row below).

### 3.2 Is S - U just "U is stale"?
Partly, for points. U is a deliberately wrong input (it believes the sitter plays), so any sensible upward response helps. A
constant 6.6% scale on every teammate (label-free, from 2023) recovers about 92% of the pts gain. S - A is -0.0018
[-0.0118, +0.0092]: the CRPS cannot tell production's per-player allocation from a uniform scale for points. The within-event
slope (1.13 [0.50, 1.72]) does say the allocation points the right way. So "who gains points" is directionally right, but its
CRPS advantage over "everyone gets a bit more" is unmeasured at this n.
For reb and ast, a uniform scale does nothing or hurts (A - U +0.0009 / +0.0030), and S beats it (-0.0063 / -0.0078, CIs
exclude 0). Their gain is allocation, consistent with the same-position slice (reb same-position -0.0088 vs other -0.0035).
The rebound and assist mechanism is the better-supported one, even though reb's size is fragile.

### 3.3 The review's biggest risk: does the gain live in k = 1?
Yes. pts k = 1 is -0.0346 [-0.0520, -0.0175] and k = 2-3 is -0.0123 [-0.0297, +0.0047]. reb k = 1 is -0.0115 and k = 2-3 is
+0.0004. This favours the display: k = 1 is where the teammates' recency features still reflect games with the sitter, as on a
k = 0 display night. The placebo (1b) is itself a k = 0 night and shows a false vacancy costs +0.048 pts. The cost of
displaying a scenario that does not happen is real and larger than the gain when it does. That is a copy point for the app,
not a defect.

### 3.4 Missingness and conditioning on "teammate played"
Rows are identical across arms, so selection cannot favour one arm on the scored set. The open question was whether a sitter's
absence pulls extra low-minute players onto the scored set. It does not, measurably. Played teammates per team-game are 10.66
on event nights vs 10.71 otherwise, and zero-minute rows are 2.31 vs 2.68 (2024, 629 vs 2,011 team-games). The rule already
discloses that the scenario does not model P(play) and that DNP rows are out of scope. No NULL pattern is involved: U fills a
reported team-game with nobody rotation-OUT with explicit zeros, as production does, and every event game has a report
(`has_report == 1` enforced).

## 4. Per-stat verdicts

- **pts: SURVIVES.** The placebo reverses, 2023 replicates, every month-drop holds, top-5 sitter-seasons and top-5 teams leave
  the sign (top-5 teams' CI touches 0), the gain is carried by k = 1 (the display case), and it is on the integer grid.
  Condition to state with it: the measured effect (-0.023) is at roughly the 80%-power MDE (about 0.021 from `g2_run.json` se
  0.0075), so it is likely inflated by selection on significance (the season-start head gives -0.032; the truth is plausibly
  -0.01 to -0.03). The per-player allocation is not shown to beat a uniform scale in CRPS (3.2).
- **reb: WOUNDED.** The direction is real (placebo +0.018, allocation beats naive, k = 1 -0.0115 in 2024). The floor-sized
  magnitude is not robust: top-5 sitter-seasons carry it (53%, G-TOP5 flag confirmed), one month or one team removed takes the
  point above the floor, 2023 and k = 2-3 do not support it, and the observed effect is below the MDE. Honest reading: "reb
  scenario points the right way; the size of the benefit is uncertain and concentrated in a few sitters."
- **ast: INCONCLUSIVE stands.** Placebo +0.0125 (reverses) and allocation beats naive, so the mechanism is real. The point,
  -0.0048, misses the floor by 0.0002. No change.
- **fg3m: UNRESOLVED stands.** Placebo +0.0016 [-0.0003, +0.0036] (the gain vanishes). The floor is unreachable by design.
- **Slopes (RIGHT_SIZED).** Not attacked as a separate claim. Decomposition: the pooled slope (0.70 pts) mixes a within-event
  allocation slope near 1 and a between-event team-total slope of 0.10 (2024) and 0.50 (2023), both without a CI. The "right
  size" is driven by who gains, not by how much the team total moves. Descriptive only, not registered.

## 7. Playbook check (gradient-boosted trees)

1. **Information or capacity:** information already in production (the report's OUT set). The test asks whether the response to
   it is right. No new capacity.
2. **Enough data for the class:** heads train on 2022 onward (about 1-2 seasons by block). That is adequate for trees per the
   playbook. A 2023 weakness (reb) is weak evidence on its own, but here it agrees with the 2024 knockouts.
3. **Test validity:** real tips (1,504/1,504 and lead >= 60 min), integer support on both arms, played-rows handling identical
   across arms, and a planted future that passes. The label-shuffle null is invalid for a shift contrast (3.1). The placebo
   sitter is the valid one and passes. The comparator (U) deliberately lacks information production has; that is the question
   being asked, not a flaw, but it makes "HELPS" easy for any sensible response (3.2).
4. **Lineage:** T092-T095 heads; T476 (under-reaction prior: mean residual under U +0.72 pts vs mean delta +0.60, so S closes
   about 83% of the gap, consistent); T499 (pts leader-out gains failed in one season, the same fragility class as reb here);
   the opp_adjusted_ridge NULL-pattern precedent (checked: no NULL asymmetry; zeros are explicit production semantics).
5. **Scored against:** integer CRPS against the stale-input arm, not against a market line. Nothing here speaks to betting value.

Class checks: missingness beyond as-of (3.4, pass); planted future (pass, extended); does the arm only re-express the base?
For pts, largely yes: a constant scale matches it (3.2). For reb and ast, no.
