# Results — Possession Sim, Player Props & Model Routing (2026-10-08)

_Autonomous run. 4 real seasons (2022-23 … 2025-26), ~5,300 games, 1.05M parsed possessions.
Every number below is from the real DuckDB, not the fixture. All comparisons are paired
per-player-game bootstrap with 95% CIs. "Beats" means the CI excludes 0._

## One-paragraph summary
We gave the possession simulation two things it was missing — **real 5-man lineups** (parsed
for 100% of 1.05M possessions) and **real player attributes** (position/height/weight/draft/age
for all 891 players). With those, the sim now **beats the season-average baseline on rebounds**
(first player-prop stat to do so with a CI excluding zero), **ties** on points and assists, and —
most importantly — a routing analysis shows **the sim's edge is systematically concentrated in
cold-start / low-sample / volatile players**, exactly where a season average has little to work
with. That is the first evidence that the generative sim earns its keep for a real, identifiable
slice of predictions, not just "in principle."

---

## 1. Did real lineups + player attributes help? (player props)

Possession sim's per-player distributions vs. a season-average baseline, 600 games, CRPS
(lower = better). Negative delta = sim wins.

| Stat | CRPS sim | CRPS season-avg | Δ (sim − savg) [95% CI] | Verdict | Mean bias (target ±0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rebounds** | 1.356 | 1.370 | **−0.014 [−0.024, −0.003]** | **sim beats season-avg** ✅ | −0.12 ✅ |
| Points | 3.239 | 3.229 | +0.011 [−0.019, +0.043] | tie | −0.01 ✅ |
| Assists | 0.869 | 0.875 | −0.006 [−0.014, +0.002] | tie (leans sim) | +0.29 ✅ |

**What changed it:** loading real `players_static` (positions) flipped rebounds from a tie
(−0.006, CI crossed 0) to a **clear win** (CI excludes 0). That's intuitive — rebounding is the
most position-driven box-score stat, so a real position prior sharpens the attribution. Points
didn't move (position tells you little about scoring *volume*). All three biases are comfortably
inside the ±0.5 success target.

### Negative result we kept honest: on-court usage
With real lineups we could finally compute a *true* on-court shot-share (team FGA while the
player is on the floor) instead of the whole-game proxy. We A/B'd it. It **regressed badly**:
points CRPS +0.398 (CI excludes 0). The season-share proxy already captures usage well, and the
cleaner usage×availability split over-concentrates the shot distribution once renormalized across
the on-court five. **Kept default-off, documented, flag retained** for future work.

## 2. Which model predicts whom? (registry routing — the headline)

For each player bucket we compared the sim vs. the season average on that bucket's games.
Buckets are the **Syntetos-Boylan volatility class** (how erratic a player's game-to-game line is)
and the **archetype cluster** (k=6, by silhouette, on position/size/age/tendencies). CI excludes 0
= a real winner in that bucket.

**By volatility class — a clean, consistent pattern across all three stats:**

| Volatility class | Points | Rebounds | Assists |
|---|---|---|---|
| `insufficient_history` (new/low-sample) | **SIM** ✅ | **SIM** ✅ | tie (leans sim) |
| `intermittent` (plays some games, not others) | **SIM** ✅ | **SIM** ✅ | **SIM** ✅ |
| `erratic` (volatile when they do play) | tie (leans sim) | **SIM** ✅ | **SIM** ✅ |
| `lumpy` | tie | tie (leans sim) | tie (leans sim) |
| `smooth` (high-volume regulars) | **SEASON-AVG** ✅ | tie | tie |

**The finding:** the possession sim **wins where a player's own history is thin or noisy**
(rookies, bench, volatile roles) and **loses only for smooth high-volume regulars**, whose own
season average is genuinely hard to beat. This is precisely the cold-start payoff CLAUDE.md
demands ("a model that wins overall but loses on cold-start games is not done") — here the sim
*wins* the cold-start slice. It's also a concrete, shippable **routing rule**: send
cold-start / intermittent / erratic players to the sim, smooth regulars to the season average, and
a routed ensemble beats either alone.

**By archetype cluster** (same direction, examples with CI excluding 0): for rebounds the sim
beats season-avg on clusters 0 and 5 but *loses* on cluster 3 (route that cluster to season-avg);
for assists the sim wins clusters 0/1/3 and loses cluster 5. The routing table is produced by
`nba.eval.model_routing.route_by_bucket` and is reproducible on the real DB.

## 3. Does the archetype MIX of the on-court five matter? (non-additive lineup test)

Your hypothesis: a five of "mix X" performs differently from "mix Y," beyond the sum of the five
players. We tested it on realized offensive output (points per possession) for the 1,154 five-man
units with ≥100 possessions.

- **Additive baseline** (mean of the five players' individual scoring rates) barely predicts unit
  PPP: weighted corr 0.17 (R²≈0.03). At this granularity, good lineups cluster tightly around
  ~1.17 PPP, so there's little variance to explain in the first place.
- **Non-additive effect of archetype mix:** statistically detectable but **practically tiny** —
  the mix explains R²≈0.016 of the residual (permutation p=0.004 vs. a null of 0.005). Mean unit
  PPP moves only ~0.02 across different archetype counts.

**Verdict:** the effect is *real but negligible* for whole-lineup offensive efficiency. Per
CLAUDE.md scope-control, this does **not** justify building the rung-4 DeepSets/attention lineup
encoder on this signal yet. **Caveats (why this isn't the last word):** it's explanatory, not
yet as-of/predictive; it's restricted to high-possession units with a compressed PPP range; and it
tests only *offensive* efficiency — small-ball-vs-twin-towers extremes, defense, and matchup
effects could be larger and aren't captured by linear archetype counts.

---

## 4. Win probability (unchanged, for context)
Nothing new here this run, but it frames the above: for **who wins**, plain **Elo** remains the
best model (walk-forward log loss 0.627, ~64% accuracy, well-calibrated; frozen-holdout 0.613).
GA-tuned MOV-Elo is the only thing that beats it, and only slightly. Fancier feature-ML and
game-context features do **not** beat Elo. The sim does not beat Elo on win probability either —
its value is the player-line and cold-start slices above, which no other rung can produce.

---

## What this run established (bottom line)
1. The possession sim **beats season-average on rebounds** and **ties** on points/assists — the
   first player-prop wins for the generative model, earned by real lineups + player attributes.
2. The sim's advantage is **not uniform** — it's concentrated in **cold-start / intermittent /
   erratic players**, giving a real registry routing rule and validating the cold-start thesis.
3. On-court usage and lineup archetype-mix were **tested and set aside honestly** — one regressed,
   one is real-but-negligible — so we don't build on non-signal.
4. Everything is leakage-tested, deterministic, committed, and reproducible on the real DB.
