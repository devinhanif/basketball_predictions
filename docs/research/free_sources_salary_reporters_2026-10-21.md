### Known-at story and leakage checks (salary)
- A contract becomes public at signing (free-agent signings from the June/July window, trades keep salary). Movers' contracts are almost
  always signed before the first game, so a season-level salary is knowable at tip for stayers and offseason movers. It is NOT knowable for:
  10-day and rest-of-season deals, buyout signings, two-way conversions, mid-season extensions (the extension salary starts the NEXT season but pages show it at once).
- Misdating risks: (1) every source above is a page as of scrape day, not as of the game; a 2026 scrape of the 2023-24 row may show a later restructure, waiver or trade adjustment. (2) Survivorship: a retrospective season table lists players who were on a roster at some point; the first-10-games cohort must be restricted to players with a signing/transaction date before their first game, otherwise a mid-season arrival gets a "known" prior. (3) Salary-dump trades: salary is the old team's number, the role is not (the very case Devin raised; leave as a slice, not a feature). (4) Cap hit vs base salary vs incentives differ by source; pick one and freeze it.
- Verification of a stored known-at: no free source stamps contract dates. Options in order of cost: store `scraped_at`, `source_url`, `salary_basis`; take `valid_from` = the transaction date from a dated transaction list (NBA.com or Pro Sports Transactions; not checked here, terms unread) and drop rows with no date before game 1 of the player's team stint; spot-check 30 rows per season against Wayback preseason snapshots of the same page (Wayback unreachable from my sandbox; Devin must do this by hand). Add the planted-future test: shuffle salaries across players within a team-season and require the gain to vanish; and the top-5-share guard (BEST_PRACTICES 2026-10-20).

### Leakage checks (news)
- Misdating: (a) a fantasy-desk item is often written AFTER the game from the box score ("Limited to 10 minutes" meaning he played 10). A regex on "limited to N minutes" will therefore label outcomes as pre-tip facts. Required: keep only items whose body is future tense ("will", "expected to", "on a minutes restriction") AND whose stamp is before the official tip time, and compare N to the realised minutes only afterwards. (b) Edits: CBS showed no "updated" marker, but a page can be rewritten; an archived copy would be needed to prove the first text. (c) Republishing lag between RotoWire and CBS. (d) Date-only stamps (RotoWire's own site) cannot satisfy known-at.
- Verification of a stored known-at: store `first_seen_at` from our own capture (the only trustworthy clock), `published_at` from the source, and `tip_at`; require first_seen_at <= tip - 60 min for the T-60 arm. For retrospective rows there is no first_seen_at, so every retrospective row is only a candidate until an archived copy dated before tip exists. Planted-future test: shift every item's timestamp +24 h and require the gain to vanish; and require the cap rows to beat a "returned from absence in the last game" rule built from our own box scores (see below).

If it shows a large minutes discount, the live reporter log is worth its cost; if not, the cap rule's expected value is small.

## Playbook check
Class: cold-start prior (salary) and an information source (news). (1) Information or capacity: new pre-tip information, but weak and mostly redundant with last-season minutes for anyone who played. (2) Enough data for this class: small cohorts (115 mover-players in 2024; rookies about 60-80); gate to be measured on the real DB before any freeze, not done here. (3) Test validity: retrospective tables built from scrapes are as-of-scrape-day, so they fail the known-at test unless a dated transaction exists; headline matching mislabels post-game recaps. (4) Lineage: T500 guards inherited (player-season clustering, top-5 share, no-harm for stayers). (5) Scored against: A0 production under integer CRPS with the pts/reb/ast/fg3m families; for caps, against the returnee-from-absence rule.
